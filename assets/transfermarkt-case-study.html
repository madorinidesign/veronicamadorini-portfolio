02 — The Problem

Great data. Poor experience.

Despite housing the most comprehensive football database on the internet, Transfermarkt was haemorrhaging users to cleaner competitors. The data was irreplaceable — the experience wasn't.

⚡

Ad overload

Up to 5–7 ad units per screen on desktop. Intrusive banners broke reading flow, interrupted table scanning and caused accidental clicks on mobile.

📐

No visual hierarchy

All sections competed for attention equally. Primary content — transfers, market values — was buried under secondary widgets and promotional boxes.

📱

Unusable on mobile

Tables overflowed horizontally. Navigation was hidden behind small icons. Tapping links was imprecise due to dense layouts not adapted for touch.

"I use Transfermarkt every day during transfer windows, but I always feel exhausted after five minutes. Every time I scroll, something blinks or pops."

03 — Research & Discovery

Listening before designing

I ran a mixed-methods research sprint to ground every design decision in real user behaviour, not assumptions.

Methods used

01

Heuristic evaluation

Mapped against Nielsen's 10 principles. Found 23 violations — highest severity around error prevention and aesthetic minimalism.

02

User interviews

8 participants aged 18–45. Mix of casual fans, scouts, journalists and fantasy football managers. 45-min sessions each.

03

Competitor analysis

Benchmarked against Sofascore, FBref, WhoScored and BBC Sport. Identified information architecture patterns that reduce cognitive load.

04

Survey (n=156)

Quantitative data on feature priorities, session duration, device usage and main frustrations. 68% cited ad distraction as #1 issue.

Key insights

🧭

Users scan, not read

Heat maps showed 80% of interactions happened in the top 60% of any page. Below-fold content was consistently ignored.

📊

Data density ≠ data clarity

Users wanted access to deep stats, but they wanted them on demand — not front-loaded onto every screen simultaneously.

🏟️

Trust through imagery

Sites with high-quality football photography scored significantly higher on perceived credibility, even when data was identical.

⚽

Mobile is the match-day device

58% of users primarily accessed Transfermarkt on mobile, especially during live matches and right after transfer announcements.

"

I'd love to just see the transfers without wading through a sea of pop-ups. The information is there — I just can't get to it without feeling attacked by the page.

— Participant 4, 28, Casual fan & fantasy football player

04 — Design Process

From chaos to clarity

The process followed a double-diamond framework — diverging wide in research and ideation, then converging sharply on the most impactful solutions.

Week 1–2
Empathise & Define
User interviews, heuristic evaluation, survey distribution. Synthesised findings into 3 core user personas and a prioritised problem list.
Week 3–4
Information Architecture
Card sorting sessions with 12 participants led to a restructured navigation. Reduced primary nav items from 11 to 7. Created a site map and user flow diagrams.
Week 5–6
Lo-fi Wireframes
Rapid sketching (80+ concepts) narrowed to 15 digital wireframes in Figma. First usability test round confirmed improved scanability but flagged mobile nav issues.
Week 7–9
Visual Design System
Built a component library with 40+ elements. Established a refined green palette, typographic scale with DM Sans, and responsive grid system for desktop and mobile.
Week 10–12
Hi-fi Prototype & Testing
Full interactive prototype tested with 10 participants via Maze. Task completion rate increased from 61% to 89% versus original site. Iterated on 6 components post-testing.

06 — Key Design Decisions

Why we made each call

01

Ads moved to sidebar & post-scroll

Banner ads were repositioned out of the primary content stream. One sponsored unit appears below the fold. This reduced bounce rate in usability tests while preserving revenue potential.

Information Architecture
02

Forest green nav, sand body

The dark green navigation creates a strong visual anchor. The warm sand background reduces eye strain on data-heavy pages compared to pure white, based on user testing feedback.

Visual Design
03

Progressive disclosure for stats

Deep statistics are one tap/click away but not front-loaded. Players see a clean card with key numbers; power users can expand into the full data table. Reduces overwhelm for casual fans.

Interaction Design
04

Sticky bottom nav on mobile

A persistent four-item bottom navigation bar (Home, Transfers, Search, Profile) keeps core actions always reachable with the thumb. Mirrors native app conventions users already know.

Mobile UX
05

News ticker as scoped feed

The news ticker was redesigned as a filterable, categorised feed. Users can toggle between Transfer Rumours, Confirmed Deals, and Match Reports — reducing irrelevant content noise.

Content Strategy
06

Imagery as a first-class element

High-quality match photography was introduced in hero areas and article previews. Research showed trust and perceived quality scores increased significantly when imagery matched the content tone.

12 — Reflection

What I learned

This project reinforced something I believe deeply: data-rich platforms don't need to be visually overwhelming. The challenge was not to remove information — Transfermarkt's value is its depth — but to make every piece of information earnable. Progressive disclosure was the key.

Working on a product used by millions of football fans also reminded me that design decisions carry real stakes. The ad reduction strategy required understanding both the user's needs and the product's business model. A design that doesn't work commercially isn't a good design.

If I were to continue this project, I would run a full A/B test on the ad placement strategy to validate revenue-neutral impact, conduct accessibility testing with screen-reader users, and prototype a personalised home feed based on favourite teams.

If I had more time

Personalised feed

A home feed filtered by favourite clubs and competitions would dramatically increase daily return rates.

Native mobile app

Given that 58% of users are on mobile, a dedicated app with push notifications for transfer alerts would be high-impact.

A/B test ad variants

Validate that the new ad placement maintains revenue while improving engagement — the business case for the redesign.

Dark mode

A dark theme using the deep forest green palette would serve the large cohort of evening / match-day users.
